# Concacaf Joins Criticism of FIFA's Plan to Sell World Cup Stakes
The Confederation of North, Central America and Caribbean Association Football (Concacaf) has joined the Union of European Football Associations (Uefa) in criticizing FIFA's plan to sell off stakes in the World Cup to private investors. This move has sparked widespread concern among football's governing bodies, with many questioning the lack of transparency and due process in FIFA's decision-making.

## Background
FIFA, led by President Gianni Infantino, has proposed the creation of a new commercial entity valued at $20bn (£15bn) to manage the World Cup. The plan involves selling stakes in this entity to private investors, a move that has been met with skepticism by many in the football community. Concacaf, in a strongly worded statement, expressed deep concerns about FIFA's governance and the lack of consultation with member associations.

### Concerns Over Governance and Due Process
Concacaf revealed that they were unaware of the proposal until reading about it in the media, highlighting the lack of transparency in FIFA's decision-making process. The association has been given a deadline of 19 September to opt in to the plan, a timeframe that many consider insufficient for such a significant decision. The lack of due process and consultation with member associations has raised questions about the accountability of FIFA's leadership.

## Implications of the Plan
The sale of World Cup stakes to private investors could have significant implications for the future of the tournament. There are concerns that the involvement of private investors could lead to the commercialization of the World Cup, potentially compromising its integrity and values. Furthermore, the lack of transparency in the decision-making process has raised fears about the potential for corruption and conflicts of interest.

### Reaction from Uefa
Uefa has also expressed strong criticism of FIFA's plan, citing concerns about the impact on the European football landscape. The union has questioned the need for private investment in the World Cup, arguing that the tournament is already a highly successful and profitable event. Uefa's opposition to the plan has added to the growing chorus of criticism, with many calling for FIFA to reconsider its proposal.

## Conclusion
The controversy surrounding FIFA's plan to sell World Cup stakes to private investors highlights the need for greater transparency and accountability in football's governing bodies. As the debate continues, it remains to be seen whether FIFA will reconsider its proposal or push forward with the plan despite the opposition from Concacaf, Uefa, and other member associations. One thing is certain, however: the future of the World Cup hangs in the balance, and the decisions made in the coming weeks will have far-reaching implications for the sport.
