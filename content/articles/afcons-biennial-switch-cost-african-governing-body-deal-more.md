# AFCON's Biennial Switch: A Billion-Dollar Blow to African Football
The Confederation of African Football (CAF) has been dealt a significant financial blow due to its decision to switch the Africa Cup of Nations from a biennial to a four-yearly event. A deal worth at least $1 billion in secured income over eight years was abandoned as a result of this change.

## Background
The Africa Cup of Nations, also known as AFCON, is the premier international football tournament in Africa. The tournament has been held biennially since its inception in 1957, with the exception of a few years. However, in a surprise move, CAF decided to switch the tournament to a four-yearly event, citing reasons such as player fatigue and scheduling conflicts with other international tournaments.

## The Consequences of the Switch
The decision to switch the AFCON from a biennial to a four-yearly event has resulted in the loss of a significant sponsorship deal. The deal, which was worth at least $1 billion in secured income over eight years, was abandoned due to the change in the tournament's frequency. This is a significant blow to CAF, which has not had a long-term financial deal since 2019.

### Impact on African Football
The loss of this deal will have a significant impact on African football. The funds from the deal would have been used to support the development of football infrastructure, youth football programs, and other initiatives aimed at promoting the sport in Africa. The absence of these funds will hinder the growth and development of football in the continent.

## What's Next for CAF?
CAF will need to find alternative sources of funding to support the development of football in Africa. The organization will need to renegotiate with sponsors and partners to secure new deals that will help to offset the loss of the abandoned deal. This will be a challenging task, given the current economic climate and the competitive nature of the sports sponsorship market.
