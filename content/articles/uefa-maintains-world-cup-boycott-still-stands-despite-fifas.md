# Uefa Maintains World Cup Boycott Stance Amidst Fifa Controversy
European football's governing body, Uefa, has reaffirmed its decision to boycott Fifa-run competitions if plans to sell off the World Cup proceed. This move comes despite Fifa's recent statement in support of Gianni Infantino's presidency.

## Background
Uefa initially announced its intention to boycott Fifa competitions in July, citing a loss of confidence in Infantino's leadership. The European body has now doubled down on this stance, stating that Fifa's recent statement 'changes nothing'.

## Criticism of Infantino
The criticism of Infantino has been widespread, with the global players' union, Fifpro, condemning 'a profound abuse of presidential power'. The South American confederation, Conmebol, usually a strong ally of Infantino, has also spoken out against 'repeated unilateral actions taken without resorting to dialogue'.

### Uefa's Position
Uefa's position on the matter remains clear: the European body will not participate in Fifa-run competitions if the World Cup sell-off plans proceed. This stance is a result of Uefa's loss of confidence in Infantino's presidency and its dissatisfaction with the direction of Fifa under his leadership.

## Implications
The implications of Uefa's boycott are significant, with potential consequences for the integrity of international football competitions. The dispute between Uefa and Fifa has sparked a wider debate about the governance of football and the role of its governing bodies.

### Global Reaction
The global reaction to the controversy has been mixed, with some criticizing Uefa's stance as overly aggressive, while others have praised the European body for taking a stand against what it sees as a power grab by Infantino.

## Conclusion
The situation between Uefa and Fifa remains unresolved, with the European body maintaining its stance on the World Cup boycott. As the controversy continues to unfold, one thing is clear: the governance of football is at a crossroads, and the decisions made in the coming weeks and months will have far-reaching consequences for the sport.
