# Introduction
The Premier League has long been known for its high level of competition and intense pressure to succeed. However, the 2026-27 season is set to begin with a record number of new managers, highlighting the lack of job security in the top flight. A staggering 11 clubs will start the season with a manager who has been in the job for less than a year, with nine teams having made a change since the end of the last campaign.

## The Statistics
The median tenure of Premier League managers at the start of each season has never been so low. According to Opta, the only other seasons which started with so many new managers were 1888-89 and 1946-47, the inaugural season of league football and the first following the second world war, respectively. These seasons, however, were not under normal circumstances, and the level of turnover this summer is unprecedented in modern times.

### The New Appointments
The recently appointed pair of Roberto De Zerbi and Michael Carrick, confirmed in March and May respectively, are among the 11 managers who will be taking charge of their teams for less than a year. This lack of experience and stability is likely to have a significant impact on the teams' performances, as they adjust to new tactics and playing styles.

## The Lack of English Managers
Another notable trend is the decline of English managers in the Premier League. With more foreign managers taking charge of top-flight clubs, the number of English managers has decreased significantly. This raises questions about the development of English coaching talent and the impact on the national team.

## The Pressure to Succeed
The pressure to succeed in the Premier League is intense, with clubs expecting immediate results from their managers. This pressure, combined with the high level of competition, makes it difficult for managers to establish themselves and build a long-term project. The lack of job security is a major concern, with managers often being judged on a few games rather than a full season.

## Conclusion
The 2026-27 Premier League season is set to be one of the most unpredictable in recent years, with a record number of new managers taking charge. The lack of job security and the decline of English managers are trends that are likely to continue, as clubs seek to gain a competitive edge in the highly competitive world of Premier League football. As the season gets underway, it will be interesting to see how the new managers adapt to their roles and how the teams perform under their guidance.
